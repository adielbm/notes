# Arrhenius definition

- acid: produces $\ce{H+}$ in water
	- $\ce{HA(aq) -> H+(aq) + A-(aq)}$
	- $\ce{HA(aq) + H2O(l) <=> H3O+(aq) + A^-(aq)}$
- base: produces $\ce{OH-}$ in water
	- $\ce{BOH(aq) -> B+(aq) + OH-(aq)}$

# Brønsted–Lowry definition

- **Brønsted–Lowry theory** (aka: **proton theory of acids and bases**)
- "An acid and a base react not to produce a salt and a solvent, but to form a new acid and a new base. The concept of neutralization is thus absent." (Wikipedia)
- "An acid–base reaction is, thus, the removal of a proton from the acid and its addition to the base." (Wikipedia)
- acid: proton ($\ce{H+}$) donor
- base: proton ($\ce{H+}$) acceptor
- **conjugate acid** (חומצה צמודה)
- **conjugate base** (בסיס צמוד)
- conjugate acid base pairs
	- For any conjugate acid-base pair (where $K_a$ and $K_b$ are their dissociation constants) in an aqueous solution at a constant temperature: $K_a \times K_b = K_w$
- heuristics for determining whether a species is a Brønsted acid/base:
	- has ionizable $\ce{H}$ atoms → acid
		- (e.g. $\ce{HA}$, where $\ce{A}$ is highly electronegative)
	- has lone pairs that can accept $\ce{H^+}$ → basicity indicator
	- net positive charge → acidity indicator
	- net negative charge → basicity indicator

- In a acid–base reaction $\ce{HA + B <=> A^- + BH^+}$, equilibrium favors the side containing the weaker acid and the weaker base. The [[Chemical equilibrium|equilibrium constant]] for the reaction: 
	- $K_{\text{eq}} = \frac{[\ce{A^-}][\ce{BH^+}]}{[\ce{HA}][\ce{B}]}=\frac{K_a(\ce{HA})}{K_a(\ce{BH^+})}=10^{\mathrm{p}K_a(\ce{BH^+}) - \mathrm{p}K_a(\ce{HA})}$, therefore:
		- if the product acid is stronger than the reactant acid, then $K_a(\ce{HA})<K_a(\ce{BH^+})$, then $K_{\text{eq}} > 1$, then products favored
		- if the product acid is weaker than the reactant acid, then $K_a(\ce{HA})>K_a(\ce{BH^+})$, then $K_{\text{eq}} < 1$, then reactants favored
	- e.g., in $\ce{HCl + H2O -> H3O+ + Cl^-}$ $\ce{HCl}$ is a stronger acid than $\ce{H3O+}$ and $\ce{H2O}$ is a stronger base than $\ce{Cl^-}$

# Neutralization

- **neutralization** (סתירה)
- "a chemical reaction in which acid and a base react with an equivalent quantity of each other" (Wikipedia)
- "In general, a neutralization reaction between an acid and a metal hydroxide produces water and a salt" [@Brown, 2012]

# Acid strength

- **acid strength**
	- "strength of a weak acid is quantified by its acid dissociation constant" (Wikipedia)
	- "The usual measure of the strength of an acid is its acid dissociation constant ($K_a$), which can be determined experimentally by titration methods" (Wikipedia)
	- "Two key factors that contribute to the ease of deprotonation are the polarity of the H−A bond and the size of atom A, which determine the strength of the H−A bond. Acid strengths also depend on the stability of the conjugate base." (Wikipedia)
- **strong acid**: completely deprotonated in solution
	- $K_a$ value: very large ($K_a \gg 1$)
- **weak acid**: 
	- incompletely deprotonated in solution
		- $\ce{HA <=> H+ + A-}$
	- $K_a$ value: small ($K_a < 1$)
- **strong base**: 
	- completely protonated in solution
	- $K_b \gg 1$
- **weak base**: 
	- incompletely protonated in solution
		- $\ce{B + H2O <=> BH^+ + OH^-}$

## Dissociation constant

- The $K_a$ (and $K_b$) value measures the tendency of an acid (or base) solute to donate (or accept) a proton to (or from) a solvent (typically water or DMSO) at a given temperature.
- The **acid dissociation constant** ($K_a$) of a solute acid $\ce{HA}$ in a solvent $\ce{S}$ at a specified temperature $T$ is the [[Chemical equilibrium|equilibrium constant]] defined by the expression: $K_a = \frac{a_{\ce{A^-}} \cdot a_{\ce{SH^+}}}{a_{\ce{HA}}}$
	- (also called the **acidity constant** or **acid-ionization constant**)
	- under ideal conditions, this is approximated by: $K_a = \frac{[\ce{A^-}][\ce{SH^+}]}{[\ce{HA}]}$
		- if the solvent is water, then: $K_a = \frac{[\ce{H3O+}][\ce{A^-}]}{[\ce{HA}]}$
			- where the reaction is $\ce{\underset{\text{weak acid}}{HA(aq)} + \underset{\text{weak base}}{H2O(l)} <=> \underset{\text{conj. acid}}{H3O+(aq)} + \underset{\text{conj. base}}{A-(aq)}}$
			- (this is often simplified to: $K_a = \frac{[\ce{H+}][\ce{A^-}]}{[\ce{HA}]}$ for $\ce{\underset{\text{weak acid}}{HA(aq)} <=> H+(aq) + \underset{\text{conj. base}}{A-(aq)}}$)
- The **base dissociation constant** ($K_b$) of a solute base $\ce{B}$ in a solvent $\ce{S}$ at a specified temperature $T$ is the equilibrium constant defined by the expression: $K_b = \frac{a_{\ce{BH^+}} \cdot a_{\ce{S^-}}}{a_{\ce{B}}}$
	- (also called the **basicity constant** or **base-ionization constant**)
	- under ideal conditions, this is approximated by: $K_b = \frac{[\ce{BH^+}][\ce{S^-}]}{[\ce{B}]}$
		- if the solvent is water, then: $K_b = \frac{[\ce{BH+}][\ce{OH^-}]}{[\ce{B}]}$
			- where the reaction is $\ce{\underset{\text{weak base}}{B(aq)} + \underset{\text{weak acid}}{H2O(l)} <=> \underset{\text{conj. acid}}{BH+(aq)} + \underset{\text{conj. base}}{OH-(aq)}}$


- $\mathrm{p}K_{a}=-\log_{10}K_{a}$
- $\mathrm{p}K_{b}=-\log_{10}K_{b}$

# Self-ionization of water

- **molecular autoionization** (or **self-ionization**)
- **self-ionization of water** (יינון עצמי של מים)
	- $\ce{2H2O <=> H3O+ + OH-}$
	- [[Chemical equilibrium|equilibrium constant]]: $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\ce{H3O+}][\ce{OH-}]}{[\ce{H2O}]^2}$
	- (**ionic product of water**) $K_{\rm{w}}=[\ce{H3O+}][\ce{OH-}]$ ($=1.0\times 10^{-14}$ at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$) 
		- $\mathrm{p}K_{\mathrm{w}}=-\log_{10}K_{\mathrm{w}}=\mathrm{pH}+\mathrm{pOH}$ ($=14$ at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$) 

# pH

- **pH** (ערך הֲגָבָה, רמת חומציות) of an aqueous solution (at a given temperature)
	- $\mathrm{pH}=-\log_{10}[\ce{H_{3}O+}]$
- $\mathrm{pOH}=-\log_{10}[\ce{OH-}]$
- pH and pOH depend on the solute and its concentration 
- an aqueous solution is said to be:
	- **neutral**, if $\ce{[H3O+] = [OH-]}$ 
		- (equivalently $\mathrm{pH} = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{p}K_w$)
	- **basic**, if $\ce{[H3O+] < [OH-]}$
		- (equivalently $\mathrm{pH} > \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{p}K_w$)
	- **acidic**, if $\ce{[H3O+] > [OH-]}$ 
		- (equivalently $\mathrm{pH} < \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{p}K_w$)
	- (or to have a higher **acidity** (or **basicity**))
- **Henderson-Hasselbalch equation** 
	- $\displaystyle\mathrm{pH}\approx\mathrm{p}K_{a}+\log_{10}\frac{[\ce{A-}]}{[\ce{HA}]}$
	- $\displaystyle\mathrm{pH}\approx\mathrm{p}K_{a}+\log_{10}\frac{[\text{base}]_{\text{initial}}}{[\text{acid}]_{\text{initial}}}$
- $C_0$ is the initial concentration (of the acid or base) 
- The **degree of dissociation** ($\alpha$) (of the acid or base) 
- For a monoprotic acid:
	- $\displaystyle K_a = \frac{10^{-2\mathrm{pH}}}{C_0 - 10^{-\mathrm{pH}}}=\frac{[\ce{H3O+}]^2}{C_0-[\ce{H3O+}]}$
	- $\displaystyle\alpha = \frac{[\ce{H3O+}]}{C_0} = \frac{10^{-\mathrm{pH}}}{C_0}$
		- if $\alpha \ll 1$, the acid is weak
		- if $\alpha \approx 1$, the acid is strong
- For a monoprotic base:
	- $K_b = \frac{[\ce{BH+}][\ce{OH^-}]}{[\ce{B}]}= \frac{[\ce{OH^-}]^2}{C_0 - [\ce{OH^-}]}= \frac{10^{-2\mathrm{pOH}}}{C_0 - 10^{-\mathrm{pOH}}}$
	- $\displaystyle \alpha = \frac{[\ce{BH+}]}{C_0} = \frac{[\ce{OH^-}]}{C_0}=\frac{10^{\mathrm{pH} - 14}}{C_0}$
		- if $\alpha \ll 1$, the base is weak
		- if $\alpha \approx 1$, the base is strong
