- A **capacitor** (originally known as the **condenser**) is a device that can store electric charge, and normally consists of two condaucting objects (usually plates or sheets) placed near each other but not touching.
	- We say that a charge $Q$ (assume $Q$ is positive) is **stored** in the capacitor to mean that the conductor at higher potential has a charge $+Q$ and the conductor at lower potential has a charge $-Q$. (The net charge of the capacitor is zero) 
- $\displaystyle C=\frac{Q}{V}$ is the **capacitance** (in $\mathsf{F}$, farads. defined as $\mathsf{1\ F = 1\ C/V}$) of a capacitor storing a charge $Q$ when a voltage $V$ is applied across its plates.



- The **total** (or **equivalent**) capacitance of capacitors connected:
	- (series) $\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{\text{total}}}=\frac{1}{C_1}+\frac{1}{C_2}+\ldots+\frac{1}{C_n}$
	- (parallel) $C_{\text{total}}=C_1+C_2+\ldots+C_n$
- $U=\frac{1}{2}CV^2$ is the electrostatic potential energy stored in a capacitor with capacitance $C$ charged to a voltage $V$
- $\displaystyle W=\frac{1}{C}\int_0^Q q\, dq=\frac{1}{2}\frac{Q^2}{C}$ is the work done to charge a capacitor to a charge $Q$
- $\displaystyle u=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon E^2$ is the electric energy density (in $\mathsf{J/m^3}$) stored in a region of space with electric field $E$
- (parallel-plate capacitor) 
	- $\displaystyle C=\epsilon \frac{A}{d}$
		- $A$ is the area of the plates (in $\mathsf{m^2}$)
		- $d$ is the separation between the plates (in $\mathsf{m}$)
- $\epsilon$ is the **permittivity** (or **absolute permittivity**) of a dielectric material
	- $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon \mathbf{E}$
	- $\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}=8.85\times 10^{-12}\ \mathsf{C^2/N\cdot m^2}$ is the **permittivity of free space**
- The **relative permittivity** (or **dielectric constant**) of a dielectric material is defined as $\displaystyle\epsilon_{r}=\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_0}$ (dimensionless)
	-  $\epsilon_{r}=\frac{C}{C_0}=\frac{E_0}{E}=\frac{V_0}{V}$
	- (sometimes denoted by $K$ or $\kappa$)
	- $C_0$, $E_0$, and $V_0$ are the capacitance, electric field, and voltage when the space between the plates of a capacitor is vacuum. $C$, $E$, and $V$ are the capacitance, electric field, and voltage when the space between the plates of a capacitor is filled with a dielectric material
